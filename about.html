About

Small Talk is a Burlington-only way to meet people — friends, or more — made by Stephen Davis, who writes the Btown Brief newsletter and hosts the Saturday coffee Meetup. It's one person, not a company. You can email him: hello@btownbrief.com.

What we keep / what we don't

  • Keep: your first name, birth year (never a full date), neighborhood from a short list, up to three prompts in your words, one to three photos, which of the four tabs you picked, and the email you sign in with.
  • Keep, privately: whether you're open to dating and, only if you are, your gender and who you'd like to meet. This is never shown to anyone. It only decides who appears in your dating lane.
  • Don't keep: phone numbers, last names, exact location or GPS, photos in chat (chat is text only), contacts, or anything from your Google account beyond the email address.
  • No trackers, no ads, no analytics scripts.
  • Delete everything is one button under Me. It deletes your profile, photos, his, chats, plan check-ins, and account. There is no undo and no "soft delete."

What Stephen can see

Honest version: the database is his, so technically he can read anything in it, including messages. Here's what he actually does:

  • He looks at a chat only when someone reports it, or when an automatic check holds a message for review (see below). He doesn't browse chats for fun and there's no dashboard that shows them.
  • He sees the report queue, held messages, and a gender-balance count for the dating lane (how many of each, how many said hi, how many got a reply). Not who.
  • He does not see your password (there isn't one), your phone (there isn't one), or your location (there isn't one).

If that's not enough for you, don't use the app — that's a completely reasonable call, and he'd rather you make it than feel uneasy.

Safety

  • Adults only, 18+. If someone seems under 18, report them with that reason — a single report hides the profile immediately, and Stephen deletes it if it checks out.
  • Block is instant and silent: you stop seeing each other, the chat goes quiet. Hide me from them is the softer version. Report blocks too, and a human reads it. Two reports from different people hide a profile from browse until Stephen looks. Nothing is permanent without a human.
  • Messages pass an automatic check (OpenAI's moderation tool) for harassment and the like. A flagged message is held — you see it, they don't — until Stephen releases or removes it. It's not perfect; that's what Report is for.
  • Meeting: "Ready to meet?" only offers public places and plans in town. Tell a friend where you're going. You can always bail; nobody here will think less of you.
  • We don't run background checks. Badges don't exist here on purpose. Trust the conversation, the public place, and your gut.
  • In an emergency, call 911. For Burlington Police non-emergency: 802-658-2700. For sexual violence support in Vermont: HOPE Works, 802-863-1236 (24/7).

If something goes wrong after meeting someone from here

This is written now, while it's theoretical, so there's a plan instead of a panic.

  1. Tell Stephen: the Report button (even after the fact — the "How'd it go?" check-in has one), or email. He will answer within a day.
  2. He will immediately suppress the reported profile from browse, preserve the chat record exactly as it is (he won't edit or delete anything), and not contact the reported person about it.
  3. If you want to involve police, he will give you the records you ask for about your own chat, and will respond to a lawful request from law enforcement for the rest. He will not hand records to anyone else.
  4. He will tell you what he did. Nothing here is a substitute for calling 911 if you're in danger.

How it works, quickly

  • People is the home screen. Say hi with one line (140 characters — that's the whole opener). Pass is invisible; nobody is ever told you passed.
  • They can wave, reply, or quietly say not-now. A wave or reply opens a chat. There are no read receipts.
  • Stuck? deals a card: a question you both answer privately, revealed when both are in. Nothing here analyzes the other person for you. Cards and nudges are allowed; everyone has them.
  • Plans are a place and a time — from the Btown Brief events calendar and Up For It. "I'm in" tells people here you're going; you RSVP wherever the plan actually lives.
  • Ready to meet? offers going to a plan together, one of a few public places, or Saturday coffee. After, a private one-tap "how'd it go?"
  • Open to dating is a private toggle. Five hi's a week in that lane, for everyone, so nobody gets flooded.

The short terms

Be 18 or older. Be yourself. Don't harass people, don't sell things, don't scrape. Stephen can remove anyone who makes the place worse, and will tell them why if he can. Small Talk is provided as-is by one person in Burlington, Vermont; it isn't a background-check service and can't promise anything about anyone you meet.
